# Co-circulation of dengue virus lineages in Rio de Janeiro: historical genomic insights enriched by the 2024 Epidemic

## Overview

This repository contains computational resources and supporting materials associated with the manuscript **"Co-circulation of dengue virus lineages in Rio de Janeiro: historical genomic insights enriched by the 2024 Epidemic."**

The study investigates the genomic diversity, evolutionary dynamics, and geographical spread of dengue virus (DENV) during the 2024 epidemic in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

By integrating genomic surveillance, phylogenetic and phylogeographic analyses, and climate-driven transmission suitability, this research provides insights into the simultaneous circulation of multiple dengue virus lineages during a major epidemic.


## Abstract

In 2024, Rio de Janeiro experienced a major dengue epidemic characterized by sustained transmission and a marked increase in reported cases compared with previous years.

To investigate the viral and environmental context of this outbreak, we conducted targeted genomic surveillance and generated **359 complete dengue virus genomes**, including **172 DENV-1 and 187 DENV-2 sequences**, sampled across multiple mesoregions.

Phylogenetic analyses identified the exclusive circulation of DENV-1 genotype V alongside the simultaneous presence of two DENV-2 genotypes (II and III), revealing extensive lineage co-circulation within a single epidemic wave.

Spatiotemporal reconstruction indicated early co-detection of these lineages across highly connected urban regions, followed by widespread dissemination during the exponential growth phase of the epidemic.

Phylogeographic analyses supported multiple viral introductions into Rio de Janeiro, primarily from other regions of Brazil, followed by sustained local transmission and regional spread.

To contextualize these dynamics, we compared dengue incidence with climate-driven transmission suitability, observing significant associations with a two-month temporal lag, consistent with a delayed epidemiological response to favorable environmental conditions.

Together, these findings provide a detailed characterization of concurrent lineage dynamics during a large urban dengue epidemic and illustrate how integrating genomic and eco-epidemiological data can support the interpretation of transmission patterns in settings experiencing sustained arboviral circulation.

## Research Objectives

- Characterize the genetic diversity of dengue viruses circulating in Rio de Janeiro during the 2024 epidemic.
- Identify the circulating DENV-1 and DENV-2 genotypes.
- Investigate the evolutionary and spatiotemporal dynamics of viral lineages.
- Reconstruct potential introductions and patterns of regional dissemination.
- Evaluate the relationship between climate-driven transmission suitability and dengue incidence.

## Analytical Approaches

The study combines several complementary approaches:

**1. Genomic Surveillance**

Characterization of complete DENV-1 and DENV-2 genomes obtained from the 2024 epidemic.

**2. Phylogenetic Analysis**

Reconstruction of evolutionary relationships and identification of circulating viral genotypes and lineages.

**3. Phylodynamic and Spatiotemporal Analysis**

Investigation of temporal patterns of viral diversification and dissemination throughout the epidemic.

**4. Phylogeographic Reconstruction**

Inference of viral introductions, geographic transitions, and regional transmission patterns.

**5. Eco-Epidemiological Analysis**

Investigation of associations between dengue incidence and climate-driven transmission suitability, including temporal lag effects.

## Key Findings

- Concurrent circulation of DENV-1 genotype V and DENV-2 genotypes II and III.
- Early co-detection of multiple dengue virus lineages across highly connected urban regions.
- Evidence supporting multiple viral introductions, primarily from other Brazilian regions.
- Sustained local transmission and regional dissemination during the epidemic.
- Significant associations between dengue incidence and climate-driven transmission suitability with a two-month temporal lag.
